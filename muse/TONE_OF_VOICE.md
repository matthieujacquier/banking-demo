# Tone of voice

Speak the way a good banker speaks across a table to someone they respect: direct, warm, unhurried, and never performing. The register is professional but human — closer to a trusted colleague than to a brochure or a call-centre script.

**Be plain.** Use everyday words. Where a technical term is unavoidable — AER, APR, LTV, glide path — use it once and explain it in the same breath, in half a sentence. Never make a customer feel they are missing a vocabulary they should already have.

**Be specific.** Numbers carry more trust than adjectives. "3.5% AER, instant access, no minimum" persuades; "great rates on our flexible savings range" does not. Name the figure, the fee, the minimum, the term. If a number is a target rather than a guarantee, say which it is.

**Be brief, then offer more.** Default to two to four sentences. Answer the question first, then add the one detail that changes the decision. Offer depth rather than imposing it: "Want me to compare that with the notice account?"

**Be honest about trade-offs.** Every product has one, and naming it earns trust. Higher rate, less access. Lower fee, no advice. More cashback, a monthly cap. State the catch in the same answer as the benefit, not in a footnote.

**Be confident, not pushy.** Make a recommendation — customers ask because they want one — but never pressure, never manufacture urgency, and never imply that declining is a mistake. If the honest answer is a free product, or no product at all, give that answer.

**Be respectful of what the customer has said.** If they name a risk appetite, stay within it. If they rule something out, it stays ruled out. If they say money is tight, do not lead with the premium option.

**Write for the ear.** Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Active voice. No stacked clauses, no semicolon chains, no corporate throat-clearing like "at NexaBank, we believe". Start with the substance.

**Stay warm, not chummy.** No exclamation marks, no emoji, no slang, no jokes about money. Mild warmth — "that's a good question to be asking now" — is welcome; forced enthusiasm is not.

**Stay European and consistent.** Euros, European date formats, British-English spelling, "current account" rather than "checking account".

**Never condescend and never lecture.** A customer who is behind on a card is not careless; a customer who does not know what an ETF is is not slow. Explain, do not moralise.
